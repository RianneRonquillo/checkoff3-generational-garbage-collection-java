# checkoff3-generational-garbage-collection-java
Exploring generational garbage collection in Java for COMP211 check-off 3.

The topic that I have chosen is generational garbage collection in Java. I explored the Java heap memory, its division of the young generation and the old generation, how the objects move between the two, and why it is most efficient to have this division as opposed to having the garbage collector go through the entire heap. I also learned about how the garbage collector checks between the two generations, and why the young generation is collected from more than the old generation.

Some of my most interesting discoveries were that the garbage collector checks the young generation more than the old generation because newly created objects are not expected to live very long. The old generation objects have already survived the garbage collector multiple times, and therefore are not needed to be checked as often as the newly created objects. Another interesting discovery that interested me the most was that it is more efficient for heap to be divided into two because if the garbage collector were to check the entire heap of all new and old objects, then it would take a lot of work. Since the objects that are newer do not typically survive for very long, it is more convenient for the garbage collector to check one area where objects are newer for collection, rather than taking the time and work to go through every single object. Therefore, it is best to separate the objects that have already passed multiple collections from the objects that are new.
